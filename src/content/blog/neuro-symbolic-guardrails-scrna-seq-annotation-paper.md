## Structured Abstract

**Background:** Automated single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) annotation remains vulnerable to confident hallucinations when passive large language models (LLMs) or static ensembles encounter heterotypic doublets and dying-cell artifacts, while tool-using agents still override advisory warnings under blinding.

**Methods:** We developed a blinded neuro-symbolic framework pairing deterministic Stage-1 lineage-conflict gating (database-mined CellMarker 2.0/PanglaoDB panels; zero hand-curated marker-gene lists) with Stage-2 active LLM subtyping and an exploratory co-expression review indicator (Tool #5). We evaluated 42 methods across Blood, Pancreas, and frozen-rule held-out Liver ($N=91$ clusters; 2,184 evaluations: 1,986 live LLM + 198 rule-gated; 18 baselines) and performed a primary descriptive zero-retuning external assessment across 28,814 wet-lab PBMCs (`GSE96583` Demuxlet, `GSE108313` Cell Hashing).

**Results:** Passive LLMs (0.670–0.753) and 6-model static consensus (0.736) hallucinated on 81.8%–90.9% of in-silico doublets; advisory alerts exposed *Tool-Alert Override Bias* up to 63.6% (fast) and 22.7% (flagship). Hard Stage-1 gating eliminated overrides (0/22; 100.0% doublet/noise recall; 3.3% false abstention; pooled 0.912–0.967; held-out Liver 0.952–1.000), with fixed-output counterfactual analysis showing pure gating gains of +0.044 to +0.137 in override-prone models versus $-0.011$ in zero-override models. Across all 30 routine unsupervised PBMC (`res=0.80`) clusters, Stage 1 detected 3/3 $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters, 2/2 HTO-negative/MT artifacts, and 0/4 moderate 25%–50% doublet clusters, with 8/11 [72.7%] singlet-dominated specificity. Relative to cluster-aggregated `scDblFinder` (0.30), Stage 1 added +0 $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters while flagging +2 artifacts, +2 mixed, +2 intermediate, and +2 singlet-dominated false alerts (+8 review clusters).

**Conclusions:** Programmatic symbolic gating enforces abstention on override-prone LLMs, while operational value alongside droplet QC depends on whether extra caught non-singlet anomalies justify added manual review; Tool #5 remains an exploratory manual-review indicator.

**Keywords:** Single-cell RNA sequencing; Cell type annotation; Neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence; Large language model agents; Heterotypic doublet detection; Safe abstention

## 1. Introduction

Accurate cell-type annotation is a foundational step in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis, directly governing downstream differential expression, cell–cell communication inference, and biomarker discovery [1, 2]. Conventional automated annotation pipelines rely on two primary paradigms: statistical marker-set enrichment against curated databases such as CellMarker 2.0 [3] and PanglaoDB [4], and transcriptome-wide correlation against reference atlases such as `SingleR` [5]. While rapid and deterministic, both paradigms exhibit structural failure modes. Static marker-set enrichment suffers from set-size inflation ("giant-set hijacking", where broadly curated gene sets dominate over-representation statistics) and lacks negative-marker exclusion—frequently confusing transcriptionally adjacent lineages such as cytotoxic $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells and natural killer (NK) cells due to shared effector modules (`NKG7`, `GZMK`, `CCL5`). Conversely, reference-based classifiers degrade sharply under out-of-reference (OOR) mismatch when target cell types or solid-organ parenchymal lineages are absent from the reference index [2, 5].

Large language models (LLMs) have recently emerged as flexible scRNA-seq annotators by leveraging biomedical pretraining to interpret differentially expressed marker lists without matched reference atlases. `GPTCelltype` demonstrated that one-shot prompting of GPT-4 with top cluster markers rivals expert annotations across diverse human tissues [6], while `mLLMCelltype` extended this paradigm to multi-model consensus voting with uncertainty estimation [7]. Nevertheless, existing LLM annotation pipelines remain *passive* and *single-turn*: the model inspects only a truncated Top-10 or Top-20 marker list ranked by $\log_2$ fold-change, cannot programmatically query unshown negative markers or whole-cluster expression statistics, and operates under a closed-world assumption that every cluster represents a pure biological cell type.

Routine scRNA-seq clustering outputs frequently contain non-ideal populations: (i) technical dropout of canonical hallmark genes (e.g., `CD4`, `INS`, `SST`), (ii) heterotypic cell doublets blending mutually exclusive lineages, (iii) under-clustered biological mixtures requiring finer clustering resolution rather than cell removal, and (iv) dying, high-mitochondrial, or ambient-dominated technical artifacts [1, 17, 18]. Dedicated statistical doublet detectors—including `scDblFinder` [8], `DoubletFinder` [9], `Scrublet` [10], and `scds` (`cxds`, `bcds`, `hybrid`) [21]—mitigate droplet-level doublet contamination by simulating artificial doublets in reduced-dimensional space or scoring gene co-expression anomalies. We incorporated `scDblFinder` [8] as our primary LLM-composite baseline (because independent benchmarks identify it as the strongest overall Bioconductor detector with both unsupervised and cluster-aware modes) and directly benchmarked `scDblFinder`, `Scrublet` [10], and `scds` [21] side-by-side against our symbolic guardrail across both the 3-organ stress suite and two orthogonal wet-lab experimental doublet cohorts (genetic SNP demultiplexing [22] and antibody-oligo Cell Hashing [23]). Because droplet-level detectors rely primarily on geometric neighborhoods or global count statistics rather than explicit lineage exclusion semantics, they can miss asymmetric-UMI heterotypic doublets (e.g., low-RNA platelets fused to lymphocytes), super-loaded experimental doublet clusters, or high-mitochondrial/HTO-negative artifacts, and risk flagging rare valid populations when detection thresholds are lowered.

Clinical differential diagnosis provides a principled blueprint for moving beyond passive single-turn reasoning. Clinicians formulate competing hypotheses, actively interrogate positive and *negative (rule-out)* biomarkers, distinguish measurement missingness from biological absence, and enforce deterministic safety checks before committing to an uncertain label. Translating this paradigm to scRNA-seq raises four testable questions:

1. Do passive single-turn LLMs and multi-model static consensus ensembles abstain safely on heterotypic doublets and noise artifacts when cluster identifiers are blinded, or do they hallucinate confident biological labels?
2. Does equipping LLMs with active read-only database and expression-query tools eliminate doublet hallucinations, or do cognitive failure modes persist when models interpret deterministic tool alerts?
3. Can mutually exclusive tissue lineage panels be mined automatically from public marker databases—with *zero manually curated lineage marker-gene lists* over predefined organ compartment schemas—and transfer under frozen rules to an unseen human solid organ without target-organ expression tuning?
4. Once a cluster-level guardrail flags dual-lineage conflict, can single-cell intracellular co-expression serve as an exploratory manual-review triage indicator (not validated for automated deletion) for ranking candidate physical heterotypic doublets (warranting manual doublet inspection) versus synthetic and real low-resolution under-clustered biological mixtures (warranting re-clustering review)?

To answer these questions, we constructed a globally blinded 3-organ human scRNA-seq stress-test benchmark ($N=91$ clusters across Blood, Pancreas, and a held-out Liver cohort evaluated under frozen panel-mining rules) and evaluated **42 method configurations**—explicitly comprising 24 LLM arms (6 backbones across fast and flagship tiers $\times$ 4 progressive architectures; 2,184 model $\times$ architecture $\times$ cluster evaluations consisting of 1,986 live LLM executions and 198 rule-generated Stage-1 deterministic abstentions) plus 18 external and composite baselines (4 classical classifiers, 2 6-model static consensus rules, and 12 `scDblFinder`+Passive pipelines), totaling <span class="hz-math" data-tex="42 \times 91 = 3,822"></span> scored predictions. Through causal ablations, fixed-output counterfactual gating decomposition, 54-comparison hierarchical cluster-blocked permutation testing with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, strict-synonym rubric sensitivity analyses, donor-stratified validation across all 10 individual donors, real low-resolution Louvain under-clustering, multi-detector comparisons (`scDblFinder`, `Scrublet`, `scds`), zero-retuning external cluster-gating assessment on 28,814 cells from two orthogonal wet-lab experimental doublet PBMC cohorts (`GSE96583` Demuxlet [22] and `GSE108313` Cell Hashing [23]), a 180-configuration hyperparameter grid, and a 1,188-cluster contamination gradient, we characterize two previously unrecognized LLM failure modes—*Genome-Annotation Missingness Misattribution* and cross-tier *Tool-Alert Override Bias*—and quantify both the deterministic safety gains and the incremental anomaly-versus-review-cost trade-off of programmatic neuro-symbolic gating.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Multi-Organ Human scRNA-seq Cohorts and Frozen-Rule Cross-Organ Transfer Design

To mitigate single-tissue memorization and evaluate cross-organ transfer across hematopoietic, endocrine/exocrine, and hepatic biology, we assembled three publicly available human scRNA-seq cohorts (Supplementary Table S1):

1. **Blood (Development Cohort — PBMC 3k):** 2,638 quality-filtered human peripheral blood mononuclear cells from 10x Genomics (`hg19` reference, 1 donor) [11], processed in Seurat v5/v6 [12] (`NormalizeData`, `FindVariableFeatures`, PCA, Louvain clustering) into 9 canonical populations (`Naive CD4+ T cell`, `Memory CD4+ T cell`, `CD8+ T cell`, `B cell`, `CD14+ Monocyte`, `FCGR3A+ Monocyte`, `NK cell`, `Dendritic cell (cDC2)`, and `Platelet`).
2. **Pancreas (Validation Cohort — Baron et al., 2016):** 8,569 human pancreatic cells from 4 non-diabetic donors (`human1`–`human4`; NCBI GEO `GSE84133`, `GSM2230757`–`GSM2230760`) [13], spanning 11 author-annotated populations (`Pancreatic beta`, `alpha`, `delta`, `gamma (PP)`, `acinar`, `ductal`, `Endothelial cell`, `Activated pancreatic stellate`, `Quiescent pancreatic stellate`, `Macrophage`, and `Mast cell`).
3. **Liver (Held-Out Cross-Organ Transfer Cohort — MacParland et al., 2018):** 10,594 human intrahepatic cells from 5 healthy donors (`P1TLH`–`P5TLH`; EMBL-EBI Single Cell Expression Atlas `E-HCAD-9`, 96.6% Ensembl-to-HGNC symbol mapping) [14], spanning 10 hepatic and immune lineages (`Hepatocyte`, `Cholangiocyte`, `Liver sinusoidal endothelial cell (LSEC)`, `Hepatic stellate cell`, `Kupffer cell`, `Inflammatory hepatic macrophage`, `Alpha-beta T cell`, `Natural killer (NK) cell`, `Mature B cell`, and `Plasma cell`).

### 2.2 Leakage, Freeze, and Provenance Audit

Because cross-organ transfer claims can be undermined by undocumented database or hyperparameter leakage, we explicitly audit the separation between manual domain specifications, automated database mining, and frozen algorithmic rules (full SHA-256 digests, file timestamps, model IDs, and API dates are archived in Supplementary Table S6):

* **What is manually specified:** (i) The organ compartment ontology dictionary (`TISSUE_COMPARTMENTS` in `tools/bio_tools.py`), which maps high-level compartment names (`Blood`: 5; `Pancreas`: 9; `Liver`: 7; plus unbenchmarked `Lung` and `Kidney` templates) to canonical CellMarker 2.0 and PanglaoDB cell-type strings; (ii) a fixed 8-gene mitochondrial stress vector (`MT_STRESS_GENES`: `MT-CO1`, `MT-CO2`, `MT-CO3`, `MT-ND1`, `MT-ND2`, `MT-ND4`, `MT-ATP6`, `MT-CYB`); and (iii) one physiological cytotoxic overlap exemption (`ALLOWED_BIOLOGICAL_OVERLAPS`: Blood `T_lineage + NK_lineage` only).
* **What uses zero manually curated lineage marker-gene lists:** Every organ lineage marker panel (Supplementary Table S2) is populated dynamically at runtime solely by querying static local SQLite snapshots of CellMarker 2.0 (`CellMarker2.0_Human_markers.csv`, SHA-256 `9e7c2abb...`) and PanglaoDB (`PanglaoDB_markers_human.csv`, SHA-256 `19374b3d...`). Because Liver panel extraction utilizes Liver-specific curated records from these public databases given the predefined 7-compartment Liver schema, we explicitly term Liver evaluation **frozen-rule cross-organ transfer without Liver expression tuning**, rather than feature-level zero-shot evaluation devoid of organ database knowledge.
* **Parameter freeze order and sensitivity grid role:** All panel-mining formulas, ubiquitousness and exclusivity cutoffs ($u_{\text{glob}} \le 0.10$, exclusivity $\ge 3.0\times$, $K=10$), and cluster activation thresholds ($\Delta\text{pct} \ge 0.25$, ultra-specific anchor $\text{pct\_in} \ge 0.70$ and $\Delta\text{pct} \ge 0.55$) in `bio_tools.py` v2.2 (SHA-256 `6fac2344...`) were developed on Blood, validated on Pancreas, and held completely frozen during Liver benchmark generation and LLM evaluation. The 180-configuration hyperparameter grid (`scripts/18_hyperparam_sensitivity.py`) was executed *retrospectively* as a non-brittleness sensitivity check after default parameters were locked and never used to select defaults. We transparently note that the public Git repository records coarse milestone commits rather than per-experiment commit logs; accordingly, all database snapshots, frozen panels (`frozen_v22_panels.json`), raw JSONL agent traces, and scoring scripts are archived with cryptographic hashes (Supplementary Table S6).

### 2.3 Controlled Stress-Test Perturbations and Global Blinding

From the 30 valid biological source clusters across the three cohorts, we constructed four evaluation conditions totaling $N = 91$ blinded test clusters (`Blood`: $n=27$; `Pancreas`: $n=33$; `Liver`: $n=31$):

* **Clean biological clusters ($n = 30$; 9 Blood, 11 Pancreas, 10 Liver):** Unperturbed real clusters presented with Top-20 upregulated genes (`FindAllMarkers`, Wilcoxon rank-sum test ranked by `avg_log2FC` with `pct.1`, `pct.2`, and adjusted $p$-values).
* **Top-5 technical marker dropout ($n = 30$; 9 Blood, 11 Pancreas, 10 Liver):** Paired with the same 30 biological source clusters, masking each cluster's Top-5 hallmark genes (e.g., `INS` in Beta, `SST` in Delta, `PPY` in Gamma, `CD14`/`LYZ` in Monocyte) from both the initial prompt and that case's SQLite expression slice.
* **In-silico heterotypic doublets ($n = 22$; 6 Blood, 8 Pancreas, 8 Liver):** Synthesized directly from real raw UMI vectors by summing randomly paired single cells from two mutually exclusive lineages (80 synthetic doublet cells per cluster, e.g., `Naive CD4+ T + Platelet`, `Beta + Acinar`, `Hepatocyte + Kupffer`, `LSEC + Kupffer`) and log-normalizing identically to real clusters (`AMBIGUOUS_DOUBLET`).
* **Real high-mitochondrial, ribosomal, and background noise controls ($n = 9$; 3 per organ):** Real QC-rejected high-mitochondrial dying cells (Blood/Liver) or ribosomal-stressed cells (Pancreas), pan-cluster random mixtures, and low-signal background noise (`UNKNOWN_OR_ARTIFACT`).

All 91 cases were globally shuffled (`seed = 20261126`) into opaque IDs (`MT_001`–`MT_091`; Phase 1 pilot: `CASE_01`–`CASE_27`) and indexed into a read-only SQLite sandbox (`bio_sandbox_v2.sqlite`, `1,007,790` expression rows). During LLM execution via Pi Agent `v1.6.1`, all filesystem, shell, MCP, and web tools were disabled (`--no-builtin-tools --no-extensions`), exposing only four read-only bioinformatics tools (`tools/bio_extension.ts`; Supplementary Methods S2).

### 2.4 Database-Mined Tissue-Adaptive Lineage Panel and Genome-Annotation Awareness

In frozen `bio_tools.py` v2.2 (exact equations in Supplementary Methods S1), each compartment $c$ of organ $T$ accumulates curated evidence $E_T(g, c)$ from CellMarker 2.0 Normal-tissue records (<span class="hz-math" data-tex="2.0 \times \sum \text{PMID count}"></span>, falling back to pan-tissue CellMarker Normal records if $<5$ genes match) plus PanglaoDB records with `ubiquitousness_index <= 0.08` ($w \cdot (1 - \min(\text{ubiq}, 0.5))$, where $w=3.0$ for canonical markers and <span class="hz-math" data-tex="1.0"></span> otherwise). Candidate genes then pass three deterministic filters:

1. **Prefix & Pan-Tissue Ubiquitousness Exclusion ($u_{\text{glob}} \le 0.10$):** Genes with mitochondrial, ribosomal, or HLA prefixes (`MT-`, `RPS`, `RPL`, `HLA-`) and genes whose maximum pan-database PanglaoDB ubiquitousness index $u_{\text{glob}}(g) = \max_{c'} \text{ubiquitousness\_index}(g, c')$ exceeds `0.10` (default `0.0` if unindexed in PanglaoDB) are dropped automatically, removing pan-stromal and housekeeping confounders (`VIM`, `B2M`, `CD74`, `COL6A2`) without manual blacklists.
2. **Cross-Compartment Exclusivity ($\ge 3.0\times$):** Gene $g$ is retained in compartment $c$ only if $E_T(g, c) \ge 3.0 \cdot \max_{c' \ne c} E_T(g, c')$ and $E_T(g, c) \ge 2.0$. Surviving anchors are ranked by descending $E_T(g, c)$ (tie-broken by lower $u_{\text{glob}}$) and truncated to $K = 10$ per compartment.
3. **Cluster Expression Classification & Conflict Gate:** Each measured anchor is classified as `HIGH_SPECIFIC` ($\text{pct\_in} \ge 0.40$ and $\Delta\text{pct} \ge 0.25$) or `MODERATE_SPECIFIC` ($\text{pct\_in} \ge 0.20$ and $\Delta\text{pct} \ge 0.12$), where $\Delta\text{pct} = \text{pct\_in} - \text{pct\_out}$. Compartment $c$ is declared *strongly active* if either (a) $\ge 2$ panel genes are specific (`HIGH_SPECIFIC` or `MODERATE_SPECIFIC`) with $\max_{g \in c} \Delta\text{pct}(g) \ge 0.25$, or (b) $\ge 1$ ultra-specific anchor satisfies $\text{pct\_in} \ge 0.70$ and $\Delta\text{pct} \ge 0.55$. After removing the single Blood cytotoxic overlap (`T_lineage + NK_lineage`), $\ge 2$ active exclusive compartments trigger `doublet_conflict_alert = true`, whereas <span class="hz-math" data-tex="0"></span> active compartments trigger `no_lineage_alert = true`.

To resolve *Genome-Annotation Missingness Misattribution* identified in Phase 1 (Section 3.1), `dataset_genes` indexes every measured symbol per cohort so `query_expr` returns `NOT_IN_DATASET_GENOME` rather than `0.0 (ABSENT)` when a queried gene (e.g., `TRAC` in `hg19` PBMC 3k) is absent from the reference genome.

### 2.5 Progressive Agent Architectures, LLMs, and Baselines

We evaluated **42 methods** (<span class="hz-math" data-tex="42 \times 91 = 3,822"></span> predictions) partitioned into **24 LLM arms** and **18 external/composite baselines**:

* **24 LLM Arms (6 Backbones $\times$ 4 Architectures = 2,184 Model $\times$ Architecture $\times$ Cluster Evaluations):** Spanning 3 fast-tier models (`DeepSeek V4.1 Flash`, `Claude Haiku 5.5`, `GLM 5.3 Flash`) and 3 flagship models (`GPT-6.2 Sol`, `GPT-6.2 Astra`, `Claude Opus 5.5`), executed on `2026-11-27` under provider default decoding via `scripts/11_run_pi_benchmark.py`. Of these 2,184 evaluations, **1,986 were live LLM executions** (6 models $\times$ [91 Arm 1 + 91 Arm 2 + 91 Arm 3 + 58 Arm 4 Stage-2 passing clusters]; **0 API errors and 0 JSON parse failures**), while **198 were rule-generated Stage-1 deterministic abstentions** (6 models $\times$ 33 Stage-1-gated clusters: 22 doublets + 9 noise/MT controls + 2 valid false abstentions `MT_007` and `MT_041`) resolved directly by `bio_tools.py` without invoking an LLM (runtime, token counts, context windows, and model IDs in Supplementary Table S6):
  - *Arm 1 (Passive LLM / `GPTCelltype` paradigm):* Single-turn static Top-20 marker prompt, zero tools.
  - *Arm 2 (Agentic Free Tools):* Multi-turn agent with read-only `search_db`, `query_expr`, and `top_genes`.
  - *Arm 3 (Agentic + Lineage Panel, Soft Prompting):* Adds advisory `screen_lineage_panel`; the LLM retains final override discretion.
  - *Arm 4 (Neuro-Symbolic Guardrail, Programmatic Stage-1 Gate):* Stage 1 runs `screen_lineage_panel` deterministically and routes the 33 flagged clusters (`doublet_conflict_alert` or `no_lineage_alert`) directly to rule-generated safe abstention (`AMBIGUOUS_DOUBLET` / `UNKNOWN_OR_ARTIFACT`); the remaining 58 passing clusters re-invoke the Arm 3 active LLM differential-diagnosis workflow (`scripts/11_run_pi_benchmark.py` lines 172–214). To separate pure deterministic hard-gating gains from the observed score change after re-invocation on those 58 passing clusters, we also report a **fixed-output counterfactual Arm 4 audit** (`scripts/34_v231_incremental_and_counterfactual_audit.py`; Supplementary Table S11) that applies Stage-1 rules to the 33 gated clusters while preserving each model's exact original Arm 3 outputs on the 58 passing clusters.
* **18 External and Composite Baselines (1,638 Scored Predictions):** (i) 4 classical methods: `CellMarker 2.0 (FDR)`, `PanglaoDB (FDR)`, `SingleR (HPCA pruned)` [5, 15], and `SingleR (Blueprint/ENCODE pruned)` (plus `MonacoImmuneData` [16] in Phase 1); (ii) 2 **6-model static consensus** baselines (`1a` Majority Vote; `1b` Shannon Entropy-Gated $H \ge 0.9$ bits) aggregating the six Arm 1 single-turn passive outputs—explicitly benchmarking single-turn static consensus rather than executing the official multi-round iterative discussion protocol of `mLLMCelltype` [7]; and (iii) 12 composite pipelines (`1c` Unsupervised `scDblFinder` + Passive LLM and `1d` Cluster-Aware `scDblFinder` + Passive LLM across all 6 models using `scDblFinder 1.24.10` [8] with a **paper-defined cluster-level aggregation threshold** of `0.50` doublet-cell fraction, plus sensitivity at a paper-defined `0.30` cluster-level aggregation threshold).

### 2.6 Stress Boundaries, Multi-Detector Benchmarking, Donor-Stratified Validation, Tool #5 Mathematics, and Wet-Lab External Validation

Beyond the 91-cluster LLM benchmark, we conducted seven mechanistic, multi-detector, and external validation analyses using exact frozen v2.2 panels (`frozen_v22_panels.json`, zero parameter retuning):

1. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Grid (`scripts/18_hyperparam_sensitivity.py`):** Retrospective 180-configuration 3D grid crossing $u_{\text{glob}} \in \{0.05, 0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.15, 0.20\}$, exclusivity $\in \{1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0\}$, and $\Delta\text{pct} \in \{0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35\}$, plus 1D ablations of $K \in \{5\text{--}15\}$ and $u_{\text{glob}} = 1.00$.
2. **Doublet Contamination Gradient (`scripts/19_contamination_and_coexpr.R`):** 1,188 simulated clusters (22 lineage pairs $\times$ 2 host directions $\times$ 3 replicates $\times$ 9 contamination fractions 0%–100%, 100 cells/cluster).
3. **Formal Mathematical Specification of Tool #5 (`scripts/19_contamination_and_coexpr.R`):** Given raw UMI counts $c_{g,i}$ and total cell library size $L_i = \max(1, \sum_{g'} c_{g',i})$ (with $L_i = L_{i_A} + L_{i_B}$ for UMI-summed doublets), log-normalized expression is computed as $x_{g,i} = \ln(1 + 10^4 \cdot c_{g,i} / L_i)$. When Stage 1 raises `doublet_conflict_alert`, active exclusive compartments are ranked by $R(c) = \max_{g \in c} \Delta\text{pct}(g) + 0.05 \cdot n_{\text{pos}}(c)$ to select the top two conflicting compartments $(A, B)$. Within each compartment $c \in \{A, B\}$, up to three positive specific panel markers $G_c^{(3)}$ (ranked by descending $\Delta\text{pct}$, tie-broken by $\text{pct\_in}$) define continuous single-cell module scores:
   $$S_A(i) = \frac{1}{|G_A^{(3)}|} \sum_{g \in G_A^{(3)}} x_{g,i}, \qquad S_B(i) = \frac{1}{|G_B^{(3)}|} \sum_{g \in G_B^{(3)}} x_{g,i}$$
   To suppress 1-UMI ambient RNA without external decontamination priors, an adaptive per-compartment threshold is set to $\tau_c = \max\!\big(0.8,\; 0.5 \cdot \operatorname{median}\{S_c(i) : S_c(i) > 0\}\big)$ (default <span class="hz-math" data-tex="0.8"></span> if no cell is positive), yielding binary high-expression indicators $H_c(i) = \mathbb{I}[S_c(i) \ge \tau_c]$. Tool #5 computes the maximum conditional intracellular co-expression probability alongside cell-level Spearman correlation $\rho(S_A, S_B)$:
   $$\text{amb\_cond\_max}(A, B) = \max\!\left(\frac{\sum_i H_A(i) H_B(i)}{\sum_i H_B(i)},\; \frac{\sum_i H_A(i) H_B(i)}{\sum_i H_A(i)}\right)$$
   Flagged clusters with $\text{amb\_cond\_max} \ge 0.40$ receive the exploratory review flag `FLAG_PHYSICAL_DOUBLET` (warranting manual inspection and upstream droplet-QC cross-checking, never automated cluster deletion), whereas $< 0.40$ receive `FLAG_UNDERCLUSTER_MIXTURE` (warranting re-clustering review). We evaluated Tool #5 as an exploratory indicator on 330 synthetic dual-lineage clusters (physical doublets at 100%, 50%, 30% vs. 50/50 and 70/30 pure mixtures; 194 flagged), real low-resolution solid-organ Louvain clusters, and 100% real wet-lab PBMC cohorts below.
4. **Donor-Stratified Validation (10 Donors; `scripts/29_donor_and_real_underclustering.R`):** To evaluate performance when each donor from the same three benchmark cohorts is processed individually without cross-donor pooling (stratifying the existing cohorts by donor rather than adding an external cohort), we evaluated frozen v2.2 inside each of the 10 individual donors (`1` PBMC + `4` Baron Pancreas + `5` MacParland Liver; 141 donor-level clusters across valid types with $\ge 15$ cells, within-donor UMI-summed doublets, and within-donor pan-mixture noise controls), computing out-of-cluster $\text{pct\_out}$ strictly within each donor and deriving 10,000-replicate donor-level bootstrap 95% CIs.

### 2.7 Scoring Rubric, Decomposed Endpoints, Strict-Synonym Sensitivity, and Hierarchical Statistical Inference

All 3,822 predictions were scored under a `0 / 0.5 / 1` concordance rubric (`scripts/17_score_multitissue_benchmark.R`; `1.0` = exact subtype match or safe abstention, `0.5` = correct parent lineage only, `0.0` = wrong lineage, hallucinated label on negative controls, or false abstention). To prevent composite scoring from obscuring failure modes, we report **four decomposed primary endpoints** separately (Supplementary Table S8): (1) Valid Exact Subtype Match (`/60`), (2) Valid Parent-or-Better Concordance (`/60`), (3) Valid False Abstention Rate (`/60`), and (4) Doublet (`/22`) and Noise (`/9`) Safe Abstention Recalls, accompanied by a 30-lineage confusion breakdown.

To address post-hoc rubric risk transparently (`scripts/28_waveA_statistical_audit.py`), we first note that exhaustive corpus review identified 7 syntactic regular-expression bugs affecting 17/3,822 rows (0.44%; Supplementary Table S5A). Second, our stratified $N=200$ audit (`seed = 20261127`, 72 strata, 114 unique label pairs; `scripts/26_audit_rubric_kappa.py`) functioned as an **internal automated-rubric fidelity audit** checking regex scoring against explicit biological adjudication criteria (not an independent dual-human ground-truth study; Section 4.3). Because that audit revealed two overly broad Liver synonym rules awarding `1.0` to `"FCN1+ classical monocyte"` (for `Inflammatory hepatic macrophage`) and bare `"T cell"` (for `Alpha-beta T cell`), we re-scored all 3,822 predictions under two tightened rubrics—**`Strict2`** (demoting both broad Liver rules to `0.5`) and **`Strict3`** (additionally requiring explicit mature/naive qualifiers for Liver `Mature B cell`)—after verifying 100.0% (`3,822/3,822`) Python-to-R parity.

Because paired `clean` and `top5_dropout` cases share the same 30 biological source clusters (and 6 LLMs evaluate identical cases), treating $N=91$ cases as fully independent understates uncertainty. Accordingly, we computed: (i) **exact two-sided Clopper-Pearson 95% CIs** for all proportions; (ii) **stratified source-cluster block bootstrap 95% CIs** ($B = 10,000$, `seed = 20261127`), binding each `(clean, top5_dropout)` pair into a single biological cluster block ($N_{\text{eff}} = 61$ cluster blocks: 30 valid biological clusters + 22 doublet pairs + 9 noise controls); (iii) **blocked stratified sign-flip permutation tests** (<span class="hz-math" data-tex="10,000"></span> permutations flipping paired score differences jointly per cluster block) and **cluster-aggregated paired Wilcoxon tests** ($N_{\text{eff}} = 61$); and (iv) **Benjamini-Hochberg FDR ($q$-values)** across all **54 primary pairwise comparisons** (`6 Guardrail models × 9 comparators`, encompassing all four classical baselines, 6-model static consensus, and own progressive ablation arms; Supplementary Table S4). We note transparently that because synthetic heterotypic doublets are drawn from the same donor cell pools as valid source clusters, cluster-block inference quantifies within-benchmark contrast variance rather than independent cross-cohort sampling variance.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Phase 1 PBMC 3k Pilot and Causal Ablation of Genome-Annotation Missingness

On the 27-case PBMC 3k pilot (Table 1; Supplementary Fig. S1), static marker enrichment (`CellMarker 2.0`: 0.444; `PanglaoDB`: 0.481) and `SingleR` (`HPCA`: 0.519; `Monaco`: 0.667) struggled heavily with doublets and OOR failure (e.g., `MonacoImmuneData` lacks platelets and misclassified PBMC platelets as `Progenitors`; `PanglaoDB` misclassified `CD8+ T` as `NK cells` at $-\log_{10} p = 25.5$). Passive `DeepSeek V4.1 Flash` (Arm 1, 0.704) achieved 0.944 on clean and dropout clusters but hallucinated single cell types on **100% (6/6)** of doublets and mislabeled high-MT dying cells as `CD8+ T cells` (7/9 hallucinations). Equipping the agent with free tools (Arm 2, 0.852) and soft panel alerts (Arm 3, 0.870; paired Wilcoxon vs. Passive $p = 0.0477$) eliminated noise hallucinations (3/3) but still overrode explicit `doublet_conflict_alert = true` warnings on **50% (3/6)** of doublets (`CASE_08`, `CASE_18`, `CASE_24`).

Inspecting Arm 3's reasoning traces uncovered **Genome-Annotation Missingness Misattribution**: on all 3 overridden T-cell doublets (`3/3`), the agent queried `TRAC`, received `0.0 (ABSENT)` because `TRAC` is absent from the 10x `hg19` reference annotation (`0` rows), and cited `TRAC` absence to dismiss `CD3D`/`CD3E`/`IL7R` as "ambient RNA". To test causality (P0-3 ablation), we re-ran Arm 3 on all 27 PBMC cases after enabling `NOT_IN_DATASET_GENOME` reporting. False negative-exclusion citations of `TRAC` dropped cleanly from **`3/3` to `0/3`**—yet the doublet override rate remained unchanged at **`3/6 (50.0%)`** on the exact same 3 T-cell doublets (`CASE_08` `CD4T+Platelet`, `CASE_18` `CD8T+CD14Mono`, `CASE_24` `CD4T+B`). Trace analysis revealed a deeper statistical confounder: **abundant-lineage background attenuation**. Because T cells constitute >50% of PBMC 3k, background detection `pct_out(CD3D)` reaches 52%, mathematically capping `diff_pct = pct_in - pct_out` at $\le 0.32$ even when 84% of doublet cells express `CD3D`. The LLM rationalized modest `diff_pct` as ambient spillover and still overrode the alert. Only programmatic Stage-1 symbolic gating (Arm 4) achieved **0.981** overall (26/27 exact/safe, 0/9 hallucinations, 0/18 false abstentions; paired Wilcoxon vs. Passive $p = 0.0083$; vs. `SingleR Monaco` $p = 0.0041$) while reducing tool calls by 30% (2.67 vs. 3.81).

### 3.2 Multi-Organ ($N = 91$) × 6-Model Benchmark and Held-Out Liver Cross-Organ Transfer

Across all 3 human organs ($N = 91$ globally shuffled clusters, 42 methods = 24 LLM arms + 18 baselines, 3,822 scored predictions; Table 2, Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Supplementary Tables S3 and S8), every paradigm exhibited sharply differentiated failure profiles:

* **Classical Baselines Collapse on Solid Organs and Doublets:** `SingleR (HPCA)` scored 0.396 (cluster-block bootstrap 95% CI: `0.308–0.484`), collapsing to **0.273 on Pancreas** where endocrine Beta, Delta, and Gamma islet cells were misclassified as `Neurons`; `SingleR (Blueprint)` scored 0.440 (`0.352–0.528`). `CellMarker 2.0 (FDR)` (0.533 [`0.445–0.621`]) and `PanglaoDB (FDR)` (0.582 [`0.506–0.659`]) recalled only **4.5% (1/22)** of doublets (23–25/31 negative-control hallucinations).
* **Passive LLMs and 6-Model Static Consensus Fail on Doublets Across Tiers:** In Arm 1, neither fast-tier (`0.687–0.720`) nor flagship models (`0.670–0.753`) reliably detected doublets (recall `9.1%–18.2%`; 19–22/31 hallucinations). Aggregating all 6 passive models via **6-Model Static Consensus Majority Vote (`1a`)** scored **0.736** (`95% CI: 0.687–0.786`; doublet recall **13.6% [3/22]**, 19/31 hallucinations) because all 6 models converged on the transcriptionally louder doublet partner. Shannon entropy gating (`1b`, $H \ge 0.9$ bits) reduced overall score to **0.670** (`0.582–0.758`), raising doublet recall only to 36.4% while triggering **23.3% (14/60)** false abstentions on valid clusters.
* **Neuro-Symbolic Guardrail Yields High Observed Scores Across Both Fast and Flagship Backbones:** Under Arm 4, all three fast-tier backbones achieved observed benchmark scores of **0.951 (`DeepSeek V4.1 Flash`, cluster-block 95% CI `0.901–0.989`)**, **0.956 (`Claude Haiku 5.5`, `0.918–0.989`)**, and **0.962 (`GLM 5.3 Flash`, `0.912–1.000`)**, alongside flagship backbones spanning **0.912–0.967** (**`Claude Opus 5.5`: 0.912 [`0.852–0.962`]**; **`GPT-6.2 Sol`: 0.962 [`0.923–0.995`]**; **`GPT-6.2 Astra`: 0.967 [`0.934–1.000`]**; reported as observed benchmark scores without formal tier-equivalence testing). Decomposing endpoints across the 6 Arm 4 models (Supplementary Table S8) showed **Valid Exact Subtype Match** of **78.3%–93.3%** (`47–56/60`; `90.0%–93.3%` excluding `Opus`), **Valid Parent-or-Better Concordance** of **95.0%–96.7%** (`57–58/60`), **False Abstention Rate** of **3.3%** (`2/60`, exact Clopper-Pearson 95% CI `0.4%–11.5%`), **Doublet Safe Abstention Recall** of **100.0%** (`22/22`, 95% CI `84.6%–100.0%`), **Noise/MT Recall** of **100.0%** (`9/9`, 95% CI `66.4%–100.0%`), and combined negative-control recall of **100.0%** (`31/31`, 95% CI `88.8%–100.0%`). Per-lineage auditing across all 30 cell types revealed that **19/30 lineages achieved 12/12 exact subtype matches** across all 6 Guardrail models (clean + dropout); remaining partial/error rows concentrated in PBMC `Naive CD4+ T` maturation state (`8/12` partial parent) and the 2 Pancreas endocrine false abstentions (Supplementary Table S8).
* **Frozen-Rule Cross-Organ Transfer to Held-Out Liver (`E-HCAD-9`):** With `bio_tools.py` v2.2 rules frozen prior to Liver evaluation, Stage-1 symbolic screening achieved **8/8 (100.0%, 95% CI `63.1%–100.0%`)** doublet recall, **3/3 (100.0%, `29.2%–100.0%`)** noise recall, and **20/20 (100.0%, `83.2%–100.0%`)** valid-cluster specificity on Liver, yielding end-to-end Arm 4 scores of **1.000** (`Haiku`, `GLM`, `Astra`), **0.984** (`DeepSeek`, `Sol`), and **0.952** (`Opus`).

Hierarchical cluster-blocked statistical inference ($N_{\text{eff}} = 61$ biological cluster blocks, 10,000 stratified sign-flip permutations with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR across all **54 primary comparisons** including `SingleR (Blueprint)`; Supplementary Table S4) confirmed that every Arm 4 Guardrail backbone significantly outperformed all four classical baselines (blocked-permutation FDR $q_{\text{perm}} = 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$; cluster-aggregated Wilcoxon FDR $q_{\text{W61}} \le 1.4 \times 10^{-6}$), 6-model static consensus ($q_{\text{perm}} \le 0.0024$; $q_{\text{W61}} \le 7.2 \times 10^{-5}$), and its own Passive LLM ($q_{\text{perm}} = 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$; $q_{\text{W61}} \le 2.3 \times 10^{-5}$). Five of six Guardrail models (all three fast-tier models plus `GPT-6.2 Sol` and `Claude Opus 5.5`) also significantly beat `scDblFinder(C)+Passive` after 54-test FDR correction ($q_{\text{perm}} = 0.0074\text{--}0.0465$; `GPT-6.2 Astra`: $q_{\text{perm}} = 0.0720$; cluster-Wilcoxon $q_{\text{W61}} \le 0.0237$ across all 6 models).

### 3.3 Cross-Model and Cross-Tier *Tool-Alert Override Bias*

Auditing all 23 doublet alerts and 10 no-lineage alerts issued under Arm 3 across the 6 models (Fig. 3, Table 3) demonstrated that *Tool-Alert Override Bias* persists into flagship frontier reasoning models:

* **Fast Tier:** `DeepSeek V4.1 Flash` overrode **14/22 (63.6%, exact 95% CI `40.7%–82.8%`)** true doublet alerts (Arm 3 $\to$ Arm 4 $\Delta = +0.143$, 54-test blocked-permutation FDR $q = 0.0019$); `Claude Haiku 5.5` overrode **9/22 (40.9%, `20.7%–63.7%`)** ($\Delta = +0.088$, $q = 0.0231$); whereas `GLM 5.3 Flash` respected **22/22 (0.0%, `0.0%–15.4%`)**.
* **Flagship Tier:** While `GPT-6.2 Sol` and `GPT-6.2 Astra` achieved **0/22 (0.0%, `0.0%–15.4%`)** override on true doublets (with `GPT-6.2 Astra` selectively overriding only the single valid false-positive alert `MT_041` clean Pancreas gamma cell to reach 0.978), flagship **`Claude Opus 5.5` still overrode 5/22 (22.7%, `7.8%–45.4%`)** true doublet alerts (`MT_005`, `MT_011`, `MT_023`, `MT_024`, `MT_057`) plus 1 true noise/MT alert (`MT_039`), hallucinating confident single-lineage calls despite explicit deterministic tool warnings.

Because Arm 4 directly resolves the 33 Stage-1-gated clusters by rule and re-invokes the Arm 3 agent on the remaining 58 passing clusters (`scripts/11_run_pi_benchmark.py` lines 172–214), we separated pure deterministic hard-gating gains from the observed score change after re-invocation using a **fixed-output counterfactual analysis** holding each model's Arm 3 outputs fixed on the 58 passing clusters (`scripts/34_v231_incremental_and_counterfactual_audit.py`; Supplementary Table S11). ### 3.4 Multi-Detector Complementarity (`scDblFinder`, `Scrublet`, `scds`), Contamination Gradient, and Donor-Stratified Validation (10 Donors)

Comparing three established statistical droplet detectors (`scDblFinder`, `Scrublet`, and `scds`) directly against frozen v2.2 symbolic gating on the 91 clusters (Fig. 4, Fig. 7A, Table 4, Supplementary Table S9) highlighted functional complementarity rather than redundancy:

* At default cluster doublet-fraction threshold (`0.50`), **Unsupervised `scDblFinder`** recalled **63.6% (14/22; 95% CI `40.7%–82.8%`)** of doublets (`Blood`: 83.3%, `Pancreas`: 62.5%, `Liver`: 50.0%; cluster AUROC `0.9955`), missing asymmetric-UMI `CD4T+Platelet` (`MT_011`), `Endothelial+Macrophage` (`MT_091`), `Ductal+Macrophage` (`MT_057`), `Acinar+Ductal` (`MT_090`), and 4/8 Liver doublets. **Cluster-Aware `scDblFinder` (`thr = 0.50`)** raised doublet recall to **72.7% (16/22; `49.8%–89.3%`; AUROC `0.9970`)**, and prepending it to Passive LLMs (`Arm 1d`) yielded 0.824–0.896—still 6.5–12.1 percentage points below Arm 4 Guardrail. Lowering `thr = 0.30` recovered 22/22 doublets (`100.0% [84.6%–100.0%]`) but induced **4/60 false positives** (`Valid Spec = 93.3% [83.8%–98.2%]`) on clean/dropout `PBMC cDC2` and `Liver HSC`.
* **`scds-hybrid` (`estNdbl`, `frac >= 0.50`)** matched Cluster-Aware `scDblFinder` at **72.7% (16/22; `49.8%–89.3%`; AUROC `0.9909`; Valid Spec `100.0%`)**, rising to **77.3% (17/22)** under rate-matched `Rate-q94` (and `scds-bcds`: 77.3%; `scds-cxds`: 68.2%) and **90.9% (20/22; Valid Spec 96.7%)** at `frac >= 0.30`.

### 3.7 Initial External Cluster-Gating Assessment on Two Orthogonal Wet-Lab Experimental Doublet Cohorts (Zero Retuning)

To provide an initial external assessment of frozen v2.2 Stage-1 symbolic gating and exploratory Tool #5 outside synthetic UMI-summed doublets, we evaluated both modules—with zero threshold or panel retuning—alongside `scDblFinder`, `Scrublet`, and `scds` across **28,814 real human PBMCs** from two orthogonal wet-lab experimental doublet cohorts: genetic SNP demultiplexing (`GSE96583` Kang et al., 14,619 cells, 8 donors, 1,598 Demuxlet doublets [22]) and antibody-oligo Cell Hashing (`GSE108313` Stoeckius et al., 14,195 human cells, 8 hashtags, 2,411 HTO doublets [23]; Fig. 7B–C, Table 5, Supplementary Tables S10A–S10C). We also emphasize that these external PBMC cohorts evaluate cluster-level Stage-1 symbolic gating and exploratory Tool #5 manual-review triage descriptively (with wide exact Clopper-Pearson 95% CIs reflecting modest cluster denominators), whereas full end-to-end LLM annotation remains evaluated on the 3-organ $N = 91$ benchmark.

## 4. Discussion

This study investigates why passive single-turn LLM annotation (`GPTCelltype` / static multi-model consensus) and unconstrained tool-using agents fail under blinded scRNA-seq quality perturbations, and characterizes both the gains and boundary conditions of decoupling deterministic symbolic gating from LLM semantic reasoning across three human organs and six model families.

### 4.1 Mechanistic Insights for Biomedical AI Agents

Three mechanistic findings extend beyond scRNA-seq annotation. First, **single-turn static multi-model consensus does not remedy input-level blindness**. Aggregating six diverse fast and flagship LLMs via 6-model static consensus scored only 0.736 (doublet recall 13.6%) because every model saw the same truncated Top-20 marker table and independently converged on the higher-UMI lineage partner, yielding *correlated overconfident hallucinations*. We note explicitly that we benchmarked single-turn static consensus over passive predictions rather than executing the official multi-round iterative discussion protocol of `mLLMCelltype` [7], whose iterative deliberation warrants separate evaluation under blinded doublet stress. Entropy gating ($H \ge 0.9$ bits) proved counterproductive (0.670), penalizing benign nomenclature granularity differences on valid clusters (14/60 false abstentions).

Second, **Genome-Annotation Missingness Misattribution** highlights a pervasive pitfall when biomedical agents perform negative ("rule-out") reasoning on tabular omics matrices: absent gene rows conflate biological non-expression with reference-annotation omission (`TRAC` in `hg19` PBMC 3k). While `NOT_IN_DATASET_GENOME` tagging eliminated false `TRAC` citations (`3/3 -> 0/3`), our causal ablation demonstrated that **abundant-lineage background attenuation** independently suppresses $\Delta\text{pct}$ whenever a lineage exceeds half the tissue population (also observed in single-donor Liver `P3TLH`, where 86.3% hepatocytes attenuated `Hepatocyte+Kupffer` detection).

Third, **Tool-Alert Override Bias persists across capability tiers, and counterfactual attribution clarifies where hard gating actually adds value**. Scaling from economical fast-tier models to flagship reasoning models did not guarantee faithful compliance with advisory tool warnings: flagship `Claude Opus 5.5` still overrode 22.7% (5/22) of deterministic doublet alerts. Separating deterministic Stage-1 safety gating from Stage-2 semantic reasoning (Arm 4) lifted fast-tier models to observed benchmark scores of `0.951–0.962` alongside flagship models spanning `0.912–0.967` (`Claude Opus 5.5`: `0.912`, `GPT-6.2 Sol`: `0.962`, `GPT-6.2 Astra`: `0.967`; observed scores without formal tier-equivalence testing) while cutting tool calls (18%–28%), wall-clock latency (33%–40%), and token consumption (37%–44%). Importantly, our fixed-output counterfactual audit (Supplementary Table S11) shows that deterministic hard gating genuinely drives large score gains on override-prone models (`+0.137` `DeepSeek`, `+0.088` `Haiku`, `+0.044` `Opus`), whereas on models that already complied with 22/22 soft alerts (`GLM`, `Sol`, `Astra`), hard gating incurs a small `−0.011` trade-off from enforcing 1 valid false abstention (`MT_041`) while apparent Arm 3 $\to$ Arm 4 gains in `GLM` (`+0.033`) and `Sol` (`+0.017`) reflect the observed score change after re-invocation on the 58 passing clusters.

### 4.2 Practical Workflow Integration, Incremental Review Trade-Off, Cost, and Model Drift

Rather than replacing upstream droplet QC (`scDblFinder` [8], `DoubletFinder` [9], `Scrublet` [10], `scds` [21], `SoupX` [17], `CellBender` [18]), our guardrail operates as a **complementary cluster-stage abstention and manual-review layer whose practical utility depends directly on its incremental anomaly-versus-review-cost trade-off**. Across all 30 routine unsupervised `res = 0.80` PBMC clusters (Table 6), relative to cluster-aggregated `scDblFinder` at our paper-defined `0.30` cutoff (`5/30` clusters flagged), adding Stage-1 gating detected **+0 additional $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters** (both caught the identical 3/3) and **+0 moderate 25%–50% doublet clusters**, while catching **+6 additional non-singlet anomalies/mixtures** (`+2` HTO-negative/high-MT artifacts, `+2` standard-resolution mixed clusters, and `+2` intermediate 15%–24% doublet clusters) at the cost of **+2 additional false alerts on singlet-dominated clusters** (`GSE96583 C8, C10`), increasing total clusters routed to manual review from `5/30 (16.7%)` to `13/30 (43.3%)`. Similarly, in diagnostic grouped subsets (`Eval B`, Supplementary Table S10B), droplet detectors excelled on mixed-partner Louvain doublet clouds (`5/5` vs. `1/5`), whereas symbolic gating caught coherent heterotypic pairs (`10/11` vs. `5–8/11` at `0.50`) and asymmetric-UMI/solid-organ doublets (`CD4T+Platelet`). Thus, hard gating is most valuable when teams deploy override-prone LLMs and prioritize flagging low-quality artifacts and multi-lineage mixed clusters for human inspection even at the expense of reviewing ~20–27% of singlet-dominated clusters. When Stage 1 raises a dual-lineage conflict alert, Tool #5 serves strictly as an **exploratory single-cell co-expression review indicator** (`FLAG_PHYSICAL_DOUBLET` for manual inspection and droplet-QC cross-checking vs. `FLAG_UNDERCLUSTER_MIXTURE` for re-clustering review, not validated for automated cluster deletion), given its synthetic (`AUROC 0.985`) and solid-organ Louvain (`3/4`) ranking signal alongside substantial real-PBMC false-doublet flags (`40.0%` blind; `0/1` Stage-1-flagged).

From an operational deployment perspective, Arm 4 averaged **8.2–16.4 seconds** and **~2,380–7,285 tokens per cluster** across backbones (Supplementary Table S6), making fast-tier backbones (`Claude Haiku 5.5`, `GLM 5.3 Flash`) practical for routine multi-cluster annotation (tens of clusters in minutes). Equally important for clinical reproducibility, closed commercial LLM APIs undergo silent version drift that can alter advisory tool compliance over time; placing anomaly gating inside deterministic, version-hashed local symbolic code (`bio_tools.py`) insulates the critical safety decision from upstream API drift and readily supports local open-weights LLMs for Stage 2 naming.

### 4.3 Limitations and Future Directions

We emphasize six candid limitations that define the scope of our conclusions and guide immediate next steps:

1. **External Cohort Scope, Unsupervised `res = 0.80` Operating Boundaries, and Exploratory Status of Tool #5:** First, our external wet-lab PBMC cohorts (`GSE96583` Demuxlet [22] and `GSE108313` Cell Hashing [23]; 28,814 cells) evaluate cluster-level Stage-1 symbolic gating and exploratory Tool #5 triage, whereas full end-to-end LLM annotation remains evaluated on the 3-organ $N = 91$ benchmark, and experimental wet-lab doublet ground truth in solid organs (pancreas, liver, dissociated tumors) remains to be benchmarked once multi-donor SNP/HTO solid-tissue datasets with matched annotations become available. Second, under routine unsupervised `res = 0.80` PBMC clustering, frozen gating achieved 72.7% (8/11) specificity on singlet-dominated clusters (3 false alerts in `GSE96583`: `C8`, `C10`, `C11`), recalled 0/4 moderate 25%–50% doublet-enriched clusters, added +0 additional $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters over cluster-aggregated `scDblFinder (0.30)` while expanding manual review by +8 clusters (+6 non-singlet anomalies/mixtures, +2 singlet-dominated false alerts), and missed 4/5 mixed-partner Louvain doublet clouds (`1/5` vs. `5/5` for `scDblFinder`/`scds`). Third, in our strictly symmetric, zero-label-peeking, Jaccard-deduplicated real PBMC audit, Stage 1 flagged only 1/10 coarse singlet mixtures (`0/1` Tool #5 specificity) and blind unconditional Tool #5 scoring yielded 6/10 (60.0%; `40.0%` false-doublet flags) specificity due to genuine cross-compartment marker sharing (`CCR7`/`SELL` in naive B cells, `CD83` across B and myeloid cells, and `T+Platelet`), establishing that Tool #5 is strictly an exploratory manual-review indicator (not validated for automated cluster deletion) until lymphoid/myeloid subcompartment exclusivity and joint `amb_cond_max` + Spearman $\rho$ rules are calibrated.
2. **Single-Donor Composition Skew and Real Ambient RNA Soup:** Our donor-stratified validation across the 10 individual benchmark donors showed that while within-donor doublet recall stayed at 98.2% (53/54), unintegrated single-donor valid specificity fell to **72.5% (50/69)** across solid organs (`Pancreas`: 66.7% [22/33]; `Liver`: 77.8% [28/36]; **75.3% [58/77]** pooled when including `Blood` PBMC 8/8), and Tool #5 raised a false-doublet review flag on 1/4 real solid-organ Louvain mixtures (`81.5% Acinar + 15.4% Ductal`, `amb_cond_max = 0.7714` despite negative $\rho = -0.3538$) due to heavy real cell-free acinar ambient RNA (`PRSS1`/`CPA1`). Integrating upstream `SoupX` [17] or `CellBender` [18] ambient decontamination and combining `amb_cond_max` with negative cell-level Spearman $\rho$ are concrete engineering priorities for single-donor solid-tissue pipelines.
3. **Valid-Cluster False Abstentions and Non-Malignant Scope:** On pooled 3-organ cohorts, frozen v2.2 produced 2/60 false abstentions (`MT_041` clean Pancreas gamma cell sharing `ARX`/`FEV` with alpha cells; `MT_007` dropout Pancreas delta cell after masking `SST` plus 4 secondary anchors), and on external `GSE96583` PBMCs it flagged 1/8 real singlet populations (mature `Dendritic cells` co-expressing `IL7R`/`CCR7` alongside myeloid anchors). Moreover, all five cohorts profile non-malignant tissues; malignant tumor cells undergoing epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) or lineage plasticity can legitimately co-express cross-compartment markers and require dedicated tumor-microenvironment benchmarking.
4. **Scoring Independence and Dual-Rater Blind Human Validation:** Although our 7 syntactic regex fixes (17/3,822 rows) and tightened `Strict2`/`Strict3` sensitivity analyses were disclosed transparently—and confirmed that 5/6 Arm 4 models were unaffected by broad Liver synonyms—the regex fixes occurred during corpus inspection rather than under a pre-registered frozen scorer, and the $N=200$ audit evaluated automated-rubric fidelity against a single adjudication table rather than inter-rater agreement between two independent human experts. Future multi-cohort benchmarks should freeze the scoring rubric a priori and include blinded dual-expert adjudication of all error, boundary, and abstention cases to report human–human inter-rater $\kappa$.
5. **Predefined Organ Compartment Ontology and Small Negative-Control Denominators:** While lineage marker genes are mined with zero manually curated marker-gene lists from CellMarker 2.0 and PanglaoDB, the organ compartment schema (`TISSUE_COMPARTMENTS`), 8 mitochondrial stress genes, and the Blood `T+NK` overlap rule remain manually defined. Furthermore, because synthetic doublets share source cell pools with valid clusters, cluster-block inference quantifies within-benchmark contrast variance rather than cross-cohort variance, and modest cluster-level denominators (`22` synthetic doublets, `9` noise controls, `3` `res=0.80` $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters, `11` singlet-dominated clusters) yield wide exact 95% CIs that motivate larger multi-center wet-lab cohorts. Automating compartment discovery directly from Cell Ontology hierarchies is a promising path toward fully ontology-free coverage.
6. **Model Version Drift and Deployment Economics:** Closed API backbones incur per-token costs, network latency (`8–16 s/cluster`), and potential silent behavioral updates; evaluating deterministic Stage-1 guardrails paired with pinned open-weights biomedical LLMs will be valuable for fully offline, zero-drift institutional deployment.

## 5. Conclusions

On this globally blinded 3-organ scRNA-seq stress-test benchmark ($N = 91$ clusters, 42 methods, 3,822 scored predictions including 2,184 model $\times$ architecture $\times$ cluster evaluations), passive single-turn LLMs and 6-model static consensus ensembles hallucinated single-cell labels on 81.8%–90.9% of in-silico heterotypic doublets, while advisory agentic alerts exhibited *Tool-Alert Override Bias* across both fast and flagship models. Decoupling programmatic Stage-1 symbolic gating—powered by frozen, database-mined tissue lineage panels ($u_{\text{glob}} \le 0.10$, $\ge 3\times$ exclusivity) and `NOT_IN_DATASET_GENOME` awareness—from Stage-2 LLM differential diagnosis reliably enforced abstention across all six tested backbones (0/22 overrides; doublet recall 100.0% [95% CI 84.6%–100.0%]; noise recall 100.0% [66.4%–100.0%]), transferred under frozen rules to held-out human Liver (0.952–1.000), and showed fixed-output counterfactual gating gains of $+0.044$ to $+0.137$ in override-prone models (versus $-0.011$ in zero-override models, where Arm 3 $\to$ Arm 4 deltas reflected the observed score change after re-invocation on passing clusters). In our primary descriptive zero-retuning external assessment across 28,814 wet-lab PBMCs (`GSE96583` Demuxlet and `GSE108313` Cell Hashing; labels used solely for post hoc stratification), frozen gating detected 3/3 unsupervised `res=0.80` $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters and 2/2 HTO-negative/MT artifacts while exposing clear operating limits on moderate 25%–50% doublet clusters (0/4), singlet-dominated cluster specificity (8/11 [72.7%]), mixed Louvain doublet clouds (1/5), and exploratory Tool #5 false-doublet flags (40.0% blind; manual review only). Crucially, relative to cluster-aggregated `scDblFinder (0.30)`, Stage 1 added 0 additional $\ge 50\%$ doublet clusters while flagging 6 extra non-singlet anomalies/mixtures (+2 artifacts, +2 mixed, +2 intermediate) alongside 2 extra singlet-dominated false alerts—demonstrating that practical deployment depends on whether catching those additional anomalous clusters justifies the added manual-review workload.
